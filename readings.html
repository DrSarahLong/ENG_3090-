One short read a week

Readings

The theory behind the doing. Each week has one short piece (a couple have two) that grounds that week's concept in the field. Every one is free and open, no textbook to buy. If you read one thing a week, read the one that catches you.

How to use these. They're deliberately short and readable, the field's real voices, not dense theory. Read before or after that week's class; either works. They give you the why behind the tools and moves you're practicing. All links open freely (no login).

Foundations & the literature review

Week 1What research means in RTW + our social-justice lens
The Rhetorical Work of Research and Citation Open
Brigitte Mussack · Introduction to Technical and Professional Communication (U. Minnesota OER), 2022

Frames research as rhetorical work inside technical communication, exactly how the two halves of this course's title fit together.

Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion Open
Brigitte Mussack · Introduction to Technical and Professional Communication (U. Minnesota OER), 2022

A student-level introduction to social justice as a lens in technical communication, the frame we bring to every week.

Week 2Asking a researchable question
Narrowing a Topic Open
Ohio State University Libraries · Choosing & Using Sources (OER), 2016

A concrete walkthrough of turning a big, vague topic into a focused, answerable research question.

Week 3Searching & evaluating sources
SIFT (The Four Moves) Open
Mike Caulfield · Hapgood, 2019

The quick, canonical method (Stop, Investigate, Find better coverage, Trace) for deciding whether a source is trustworthy.

Week 4Synthesis: sources in conversation
Synthesizing Multiple Perspectives Open
Kara Clevinger & Stephen Rust · Writing as Inquiry (U. Oregon OER)

The difference between summary and synthesis, and how to put sources in conversation instead of listing them one by one.

Qualitative analysis

Week 5Qualitative research design
Research Design Open
Allison Hurst · Introduction to Qualitative Research Methods (Oregon State OER), 2023

How a qualitative study gets designed, and how you choose an approach that fits your question, before diving into a method.

Week 6Content analysis in depth
Content Analysis Method and Examples Open
Columbia Mailman School of Public Health

A short, plain-language how-to on coding text for words, themes, and concepts, the method behind your P2.

Week 7Digital text-analysis tools
Corpus Analysis with Voyant Tools Open
Silvia Gutiérrez De la Torre · Programming Historian, 2022

A hands-on, no-install tutorial for building a corpus and reading text at a distance, the same tool you'll use this week.

Week 8Writing up qualitative findings
Writing Up Qualitative Results Open
Victor Tan Chen et al. · The Craft of Sociological Research (OER)

How to organize a findings section by theme and weave in quotes, instead of dumping raw data on the reader.

Ethics & quantitative research

Week 9Research ethics & human subjects
The Belmont Report: Plain-Language Summary Open
U.S. National Commission (plain-language version, Harvard HPOD)

The three principles behind all human-subjects research, respect for persons, beneficence, justice, in jargon-free English.

Week 10Designing surveys & interviews
Writing Survey Questions Open
Pew Research Center

Real examples of how wording, order, and double-barreled questions skew answers, from the people who do this for a living.

Week 11Sampling & who gets represented
When Surveying Small Populations, Some Approaches Are More Inclusive Open
Andrew Mercer & Arnold Lau · Pew Research Center, 2023

A non-technical look at how your sampling method decides who gets represented, and who gets left out.

Week 12Analyzing & visualizing data
How to Lie with Charts Open
Jack Dougherty & Ilya Ilyankou · Hands-On Data Visualization

Truncated axes and other tricks that mislead, so you learn to make honest charts by spotting the dishonest ones.

Reporting & presenting

Week 13IMRAD & document design
Writing an IMRaD Report Open
George Mason University Writing Center

Why research reports use Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion, and what each section is for.

Week 14Revision
Revising Drafts Open
UNC Chapel Hill Writing Center

The line between revising and proofreading, and why you fix the big things (focus, organization) first.

Reverse Outlines Open
UW-Madison Writing Center

The exact technique from this week's tool: outline what you actually wrote to test whether your structure holds.

Weeks 15–16Presenting your research
Ten Simple Rules for Making Good Oral Presentations Open
Philip E. Bourne · PLOS Computational Biology, 2007

Ten quick, memorable rules (know your audience, less is more, tell a story, practice) that turn straight into a good talk.
