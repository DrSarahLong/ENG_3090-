How to turn in your notebook

Download once, submit once

  1. Open your Research Notebook.
  2. Scroll to the bottom and click "Download everything." This saves one file with all your entries and a summary showing what's complete.
  3. Submit it to the AsULearn notebook dropbox as a PDF or a Google Doc, whichever's easier. It's a completion check, so the format is up to you.

Your notebook lives in your browser, on your own computer, so downloading is the only way it reaches me. Do it at each check-in, and grab a copy anytime as a backup.

A few file tips

  • P1, P2, and P3 (the projects that get feedback): submit as a so comments can land right on your lines. Share it set to "anyone with the link can comment," or use the Canvas Google Drive submission option.
  • The final report: submit a PDF so your formatting, headings, and figures stay locked in place. (Export or "Save as PDF" from your writing app.) The final is graded, not marked up.
  • Notebook: hit "Download everything," then submit that file as a PDF or a Google Doc. Don't retype it.
  • Name it so it's easy to spot: Lastname-P1, Lastname-notebook.txt.
  • Not sure it went through? Re-open the AsULearn dropbox and confirm your submission is listed.
The one rule

When in doubt about a date, the syllabus wins. This page tells you what and where; the syllabus is the official when.

Why a Google Doc for drafts?

The projects that get feedback (P1, P2, P3) come in as Google Docs so comments can attach to the exact line they're about, right where you're working. A PDF is a locked image of a page, great for a finished piece, but you can't leave a note on line 14 of one. Share your Doc so it can be commented on, then submit the link. Your final report is different: it's a PDF, because it's done.

AsULearn

App State's online learning system, where this course lives: assignment dropboxes, the gradebook, and announcements. Every graded thing in this class is submitted through an AsULearn dropbox. (You may hear it called Moodle or Canvas depending on the year, same idea, same place.)

IMRAD

The standard shape of a research report: Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion, plus a title page, references, and an appendix holding your instrument and data. Your final report pulls P1, P2, and P3 together into this one format.
