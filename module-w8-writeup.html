A discourse-analysis paper has four moving parts. You've met them before in the lit review; here they wrap around your own analysis:

  3. . The patterns you found, each with a number and a quote. This is the evidence.

Step 3: findings need a number AND a quote

This is where papers live or die. A finding isn't "the labels felt manipulative." A finding is: the pattern, how often it appeared, and the exact words that show it. "Eleven of eighteen labels used an appeal to nature, including one that read 'gathered the way nature intended.'" Number plus quote, every time. Don't paraphrase your evidence; use the real words.

Step 4: the discussion is the analysis

Anyone can report counts. Your discussion says what they mean: why the pattern is there, whose interest it serves, who the language leaves out. Methods

The section that tells a reader exactly what you did, clearly enough that they could repeat it: what your corpus was (how many texts, what kind, gathered from where), how you coded it (your codebook), and any tools you used. Methods is about transparency, not persuasion. Say what you did, plainly.

Findings

What your analysis actually turned up, reported as evidence: the patterns, how often each appeared (the count), and exact quotes that show them. Findings are just the facts of your corpus. You save the meaning for the discussion. A finding without a number or without a quote is only a hunch.

Discussion

The section where your findings become meaning. It answers "so what?", why the pattern exists, whose interest it serves, what it does to a reader, and how it connects to the bigger conversation. This is the thinking the tools and counts can't do for you, and it's where the real credit lives.

Limitations

An honest statement of what your analysis can't claim: a small corpus, one region or one store, labels rather than the shoppers who read them. Naming your limits doesn't weaken your paper, it strengthens it. It shows you know the difference between what you found and what you'd need a bigger study to prove.
