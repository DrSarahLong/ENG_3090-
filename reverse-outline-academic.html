Reverse-Outline X-Ray

The tool breaks your draft into paragraphs. You label the research job each one does. Stacked together, that skeleton shows the gaps, the repeats, and the out-of-order sections you cannot see while you are inside the prose.

This does no judging. It splits your draft into paragraphs so you can label the job each one does for the argument (a claim, a piece of evidence, your analysis, a limitation, and so on). Then it stacks your labels so you can check the shape against . This runs on your computer. Nothing you paste leaves it. Saves only in this browser.
Claim — states a position the paper argues. e.g., "Nature appeals do more selling than any verified health benefit."
Background — context a reader needs first. e.g., what "greenwashing" means and why labels are unregulated.
Method — what you did and how. e.g., "I coded eighteen labels for four appeal types."
Evidence — a finding, count, quote, or source. e.g., "Eleven of eighteen labels used the word 'natural.'"
Analysis — what the evidence means, the "so what." e.g., "That pattern serves the seller, not the shopper."
Counterargument / limitation — the other side, or what you cannot claim. e.g., "A corpus of eighteen labels is suggestive, not proof."
Transition — a bridge that moves the reader between sections. e.g., "Having mapped the language, I turn to what shoppers actually notice."

Label each paragraph's job

One label each. Not what it is about, but what job it does for the argument. If a paragraph is doing two jobs, that is worth noticing too.

Your paper's skeleton

Now read it like an x-ray

Is there a clear claim near the top?If no early paragraph is labeled "claim," your reader may not know what you argue. A report still needs a controlling point.
Does every "evidence" have an "analysis" near it?A run of evidence with no analysis is a data dump. Every finding needs a sentence on what it means.
Is there a method section before the evidence?In IMRAD, a reader needs to know what you did before they can trust what you found.
Is there a limitation or counterargument?No limitation anywhere reads as overclaiming. Naming what you cannot claim builds trust.
Do any two paragraphs do the same job on the same point?Two paragraphs both labeled "background" on the same idea is a repeat. Merge them or cut the weaker.
Is the order IMRAD-shaped?Background and claim, then method, then evidence and analysis, then limitation and conclusion. A finding before the method is out of order.

IMRAD

The standard shape of a research report: Introduction (your question and claim, with background), Methods (what you did), Results (what you found, reported plainly), And Discussion (what it means, and what it cannot claim). Not every draft uses those exact headings, but the jobs still appear in that order. Reading your paragraph labels against IMRAD shows fast whether a job is missing or out of place.
