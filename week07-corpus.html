ENG 3090 · Week 7 Workshop

The Asheville corpus

Three texts about homelessness in Asheville. Copy each one into Voyant as its own document.

How to load these
  1. Open Voyant and choose Add Texts.
  2. Copy Text 1 with the button below, paste it into the first box.
  3. Add another box for Text 2, and another for Text 3.
  4. Three separate documents, not one. If you paste all three into one box, Voyant thinks you handed it a single text, and Documents, Document Length, and Vocabulary Density stop telling you anything.
What these are. These three texts were written for this workshop. They are not reprinted news articles. Every number in them is real and comes from the City of Asheville's 2026 Point-in-Time count: 824 people experiencing homelessness, a 9.1% increase over 2025, 334 of them unsheltered (up from 65 in 2020), and 16% of them homeless as a direct result of Hurricane Helene. The actual sources are linked at the bottom, and you should use actual articles when you build your own corpus.

Text 1 · Downtown merchants at City Council

TEXT 1news report · ~185 words
DOWNTOWN MERCHANTS PRESS COUNCIL ON SAFETY

Members of a downtown business group told City Council on Tuesday that conditions on several blocks have become untenable for their customers.

"We are not talking about people who are down on their luck," said the owner of a gallery on Broadway. "We are talking about aggressive behavior, open drug use, and human waste in our doorways. Our visitors do not feel safe, and they are telling us so."

The group asked for increased police patrols, enforcement of the city's existing loitering and camping ordinances, and a dedicated downtown safety officer. Several members said that convention organizers had asked about downtown safety before booking.

Council members acknowledged the concerns. One noted that the city's 2026 Point-in-Time count identified 824 people experiencing homelessness, 334 of them unsheltered, and said enforcement alone would not reduce those numbers. The business group's representative replied that enforcement was not intended to reduce the numbers. It was intended to protect the businesses that remain.

Text 2 · Opinion column

TEXT 2opinion · ~370 words
RENT, NOT CHOICE: WHY OUR NEIGHBORS ARE OUTSIDE
An opinion column

There is a story people tell about homelessness in this city, and it starts with the individual. He made bad decisions. She would not accept help. He prefers the freedom of the street.

Here is a different starting point. Median rent in Buncombe County has risen faster than wages for low-income workers for more than a decade. The affordable housing shortage was documented long before anyone pitched a tent downtown. Then Hurricane Helene destroyed or damaged thousands of housing units, and sixteen percent of the people counted as homeless this year became homeless as a direct result of that storm.

Sixteen percent. Not a character flaw. A weather event, acting on a housing market that had no slack in it.

The 2026 count found 824 people without housing, a 9.1 percent increase over last year. The number of people sleeping entirely unsheltered has gone from 65 in 2020 to 334 now. No plausible account of that curve runs through individual bad decisions. It runs through rent, through wages, and through a supply of housing that was inadequate before the water came.

I want to be precise about one word. We argue constantly in this city about keeping people safe, and we almost always mean keeping some people safe from other people. But housing is what makes a person safe. A door that locks is what makes a person safe. Everything we fund instead of housing is a way of managing the consequences of not funding housing.

Ask the people on the waitlist. They are not refusing anything. They are waiting.

Text 3 · Nonprofit newsletter

TEXT 3organizational newsletter · ~250 words
NEWSLETTER: WHAT OUTREACH ACTUALLY DOES

People ask us what outreach is. Here is a week of it.

We met a man at the river who had been outside for nine months. It took four visits before he would tell us his name. On the fifth he let us drive him to get a replacement ID, which he needed before he could be placed anywhere. That is outreach. It is mostly paperwork and patience.

Our case managers coordinate shelter beds, and during Code Purple we work the phones until every bed in the county is accounted for. Capacity is the constant problem. There are 824 people counted in this community this year and nowhere near enough beds for the 334 who are outside. Every person we place is a person who has a safe place to sleep that night, and we do not apologize for counting those nights as wins.

We do want to be direct about something. We are increasingly asked to move people rather than house them. When a business calls the city, and the city calls us, we are being used as a softer form of removal. The people we serve understand that better than the callers do. Case management is not crowd control. When it gets used as crowd control we lose the trust that makes the work possible, and trust is the only tool we actually have.

No amount of outreach fixes arithmetic.
