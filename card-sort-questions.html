ENG 3090 · Week 2, Tuesday

Card Sort: Strong or Weak?

Six on our food-label theme. On your own, decide: is each one strong (a question you could actually research) or weak? Then be ready to defend one call to the room.

Sort first, peek later. Don't open "Reveal the verdict" until we debrief as a class, wrestling with the call yourself is the point.

Sorted: 0 / 6

Card 1

Is organic food better?

Reveal the verdict
Weak, "Better" is undefined and the question is enormous. Better how, for whom, measured how? There's no data that could ever settle it as written. Narrow it and it can work.

Card 2

Which claims on egg cartons at the Boone Walmart are USDA-regulated terms, and which are unregulated marketing?

Reveal the verdict
Strong: to a real, gatherable set of labels, and answerable by analyzing the text itself (content analysis). You know exactly what data you'd collect.

Card 3

Are big food companies evil?

Reveal the verdict
Weak: Loaded and not answerable with evidence. "Evil" isn't something you can measure. This is an opinion looking for a fight, not a research question.

Card 4

How do App State first-year students interpret the word "natural" on food packaging?

Reveal the verdict
Strong: Scoped to a specific group and answerable by asking people (survey or interview). It hands you real, collectable data.

Card 5

What is sustainable agriculture?

Reveal the verdict
Weak, as a research question. You'd answer this by reading what others have written, not by running a study. It's a great background question for your literature review, just not the engine of your project.

Card 6

Do "regenerative" claims on beef sold in local groceries match any third-party certification standard?

Reveal the verdict
Strong: Scoped, and checkable against real certification standards (content analysis). It also pairs naturally with asking producers or shoppers, so it fits more than one method.

Notice the pattern? The strong ones are scoped, point to data you could actually collect, and often fit more than one method. The weak ones aren't bad topics, they just need narrowing before they can carry a study. That's exactly what you'll do next, in Entry 2a.

Research question

The specific, answerable question your whole study is built to answer. Not a topic (food labels) and not an opinion (are big companies evil), but a question you could actually settle with data you're able to collect.

A good one tells you what you'd go measure, ask, or count.

Scoped

Scoped means narrowed down far enough that you could actually answer it with data you can realistically get.

"Is organic food better?" is unscoped: too big, too vague. "Which egg-carton claims at one store are USDA-regulated?" is scoped: you know exactly what to go collect.
