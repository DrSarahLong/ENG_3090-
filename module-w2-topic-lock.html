ENG 3090 · Week 2 · Thursday Module

Meet the Modules, and Lock Your Topic

Your first module. We're doing this one together in class, so you'll always know how they work.

First: how these modules work

  • Read a little, do a little. Every Thursday you'll work through one of these on your own time. Short teaching, then something you actually write.
  • Your writing lands in your Research Notebook, which saves on your own computer. Nothing is transmitted anywhere.
  • Completion only. No grade on the writing itself, just that you did the thinking.
  • You only turn the notebook in twice all semester: at midterm and at the end. Not every week.

But do them every week anyway. Two reasons. First, each module builds a piece of your major project, so skipping them means building the whole thing from scratch later, under deadline. Second, together they are worth a considerable chunk of your grade (a full fifth). Cramming them all right before a check-in defeats the point: this way of learning only works when the reps are spread out.

🗣 Talk it out · #1

Say your topic in one sentence. Just one. If it takes you three sentences, that's a sign it's still too big, and that's useful to know right now.

In class we'll go around the room. On your own, say it out loud to yourself anyway, it works.

Next: what makes a topic "lockable"

You're about to commit to one topic for the whole semester. Before you do, it has to pass a simple test. A lockable topic gives you all four of these:

  • 1
    Texts you can analyze. Labels, manuals, posts, policies, ads, packaging, something you can actually get your hands on.
  • 2
    People you can ask. A group you can realistically survey or interview.
  • 3
    A justice angle. Who holds power here, who's served, and whose voice is missing?
  • 4
    A strong , the one you built in the Strengthener.

If any of the four is blank, your topic isn't wrong, it just needs tightening. That's what today is for.

🗣 Talk it out · #2

Whose topic feels too big? Volunteer one, and we'll shrink it together, from "food labels" down to something you could actually finish, like "sustainability claims on egg cartons at one store."

On your own: which of the four boxes above is hardest for your topic? Start there.

🗣 Talk it out · #3

Read your locked topic and question to the room, then answer two things out loud: what will you analyze, and who will you ask? If you can't answer both, your topic needs one more pass.

Last thing: practice the turn-in. In your notebook, scroll to the bottom and hit Download everything once. That's the exact move you'll make at the two check-ins (midterm and end of term). Do it now, with help in the room, so it's never a mystery later.

Research question

The specific, answerable question your whole study is built to answer. Not a topic (food labels) and not an opinion (are big companies evil), but a question you could actually settle with data you're able to collect.

A good one tells you what you'd go measure, ask, or count.

Discourse

Discourse just means language in use: the actual words, framing, and design of real texts like labels, ads, manuals, and posts. It's what this course analyzes.

When you study how a carton says "farm fresh," you're doing discourse analysis, looking at how language does work in the world.
