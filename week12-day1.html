A Chart Can Mislead as Easily as a Food Label

You collected it. You're the only one who's seen it. Today it talks.
Debate this

"A chart just shows the numbers. If the data is real, the chart can't lie."

Raw answers become findings in three moves

Count

Build a frequency table. How many people gave each answer? (COUNTIF does it in Sheets.)

Describe

Three numbers: N (how many), the percentage, and the mode (most common answer). Careful averaging a Likert scale.

Picture

Bar chart for categories. Pie only for a few parts of one whole. Match the chart to the data.

Start at zero

Begin the bars at zero and the picture can't lie. Cut the axis and small gaps look huge.

Same numbers. Two charts. Different stories.

What makes this one lie, and how would you fix it?

A chart bends the truth the same way a label does, usually by accident.

Where else have you seen a picture make a small thing look huge?

A label and a chart mislead the same way: by choosing what to show and what to hide. What you put on the axis, and where you start it, is the argument. You've spent a semester on labels that mislead. Your own figure has to be the honest one.
