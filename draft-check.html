ENG 3090 · Draft Check

Draft Check

Paste your draft. It flags what a tired reader catches first, coherence, academic tone, mechanics, and evidence, so you can fix them before I ever see them.

This is a first-pass digital editor that runs on your computer. It flags common, fixable problems that trip up academic and research writing: mechanics and typos, weak transitions and coherence, informal or casual language, wordiness, dropped-in quotes, inconsistent citations, overclaiming, and paragraphs with no evidence. The checks are drawn from well-documented patterns in student academic and science writing. It can even flag a few language patterns tied to common reasoning slips, like claiming cause from correlation, or overclaiming. What it can't do is judge whether an argument is actually good, whether your logic holds, or whether a claim belongs in Results versus Discussion. Those still need a careful human reader. Fix what it flags, then read the draft aloud once. Nothing you paste leaves this computer.
What it looks for
  • Mechanics & typos: spacing, repeated words, run-on sentences, confusables.
  • Coherence & flow: paragraphs that start cold with no transition, and vague "this/it" with no noun.
  • Academic voice & style: contractions, casual wording, "And/But/So" openers, "you," wordiness, passive voice.
  • Sources & quotes: dropped-in quotes, missing citations, source list-y writing.
  • Evidence & argument: claims with no support, thin interpretation, overclaiming, and causal overreach (correlation vs. causation).
  • Formatting consistency: citation style, quotation marks, spacing.
