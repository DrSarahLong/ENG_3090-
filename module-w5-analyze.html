Discourse analysis

A close, systematic look at how real texts make meaning: word choices, framing, what they include and leave out, and how they move power. You read like a detective to see what the language is doing, not just what it says. (Linguist James Paul Gee treats language as always doing something; Norman Fairclough's "critical discourse analysis" focuses on how it does power.)

Content analysis

The close cousin of discourse analysis that leans on counting: how often a word, theme, or move appears across your texts. Discourse analysis asks "how is this language working?"; content analysis asks "how often, and where?" Most good projects use a little of both. (Klaus Krippendorff's Content Analysis is the standard handbook.)

Corpus

Your set of texts to analyze, gathered on purpose. Aim for 15 to 20 items of the same kind (news articles, labels, ads, policies, posts) so real patterns show up instead of one-off quirks. "Corpus" just means a "body" of texts, and corpus linguists like Paul Baker study language exactly this way.

Coding

Tagging your texts for the things that repeat, a recurring word, a type of appeal, a framing, so you can see patterns instead of drowning in detail. On your first pass you're just noticing; later you name your patterns and count how often each shows up. (Johnny Saldaña's Coding Manual for Qualitative Researchers is the go-to guide.)

Framing

How word choice steers what you notice and feel. Call something "farm fresh" and you picture a meadow; call the same eggs "mass-produced" and you don't. From the news: "estate tax" and "death tax" name the same policy but pull opposite feelings. (Sociologist Erving Goffman named framing; George Lakoff showed how politics runs on it.)

OCR (optical character recognition)

Software that reads the letters inside an image or a scanned PDF and turns them back into real, searchable text. Your phone's camera, Google Drive, and Adobe Acrobat can all do it. You need OCR whenever your source is a picture of words rather than the words themselves.
