Week 14

Revision Is Re-Seeing

Nobody's first draft is their best one. Today we re-see the whole thing, not tidy it.
Debate this

"A solid first draft just needs a proofread. Tearing it apart to 're-see' it wastes time and usually makes it worse."

Revision is not editing. What is the difference?

One re-sees the ideas and the structure. One cleans the sentences.

Three jobs, done big to small

Revise

Ideas and structure. The big moves: focus, order, what to cut, what is missing.

Edit

Sentences. Clarity and flow, one line at a time.

Proofread

Errors. Typos, citations, formatting. Last, once the shape is settled.

Order of operations

Higher-order concerns

Focus, organization, evidence, and whether the draft actually answers its question. These come first.

Lower-order concerns

Grammar, citation format, page formatting. Real, but they come after the structure holds.

You have a paragraph you might cut. Should you fix its commas first?

Why or why not?

The reverse outline
Go through your draft one paragraph at a time and write a single line for each: the job that paragraph does, not the topic it is about. The gaps and repeats jump right out.
Topic (what it is about)This paragraph is about organic labels.
Job (what it does)Defines "organic" so the finding about mislabeling makes sense.
Only the job version shows you whether the paragraph earns its place.
Notebook Entry 14a · on your own

X-ray your own draft.

Reverse-outline your latest draft: one line per paragraph, the job it does, tagged to its IMRAD section.
Name the structural problems the outline exposed: a missing section, out-of-order pieces, repetition, a thin section.
Pick your top 2 or 3 higher-order fixes, and start at the top of that list, not with the commas.

What did your reverse outline expose?

A missing recommendation, a repeated paragraph, a Discussion that is only one line. Every draft has gaps.
