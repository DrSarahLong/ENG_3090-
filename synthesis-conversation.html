ENG 3090 · Week 4

Weave It

In class you grouped sources into themes. This tool teaches the next move: turning a group of sources into one woven sentence, the sentence-level craft behind synthesis.

This tool runs on your computer and saves nothing to a server. It uses three real findings from research on the food-label example. First you'll hear how they relate, then you'll build the woven sentence, then you'll do it with your own sources.

The three findings are the example. The move is what you'll run on your own sources for P1.

Step 1 · Hear the relationship

Two of these agree. One pushes back. Which is which?

Tap agrees or pushes back on each finding, then check. A woven sentence starts by knowing who's on the same side.

"Most shoppers read the word 'natural' as a promise of no pesticides."
Smith (2021)
"Shoppers misread 'farm fresh' the same way, hearing a health claim the label never makes."
Jones (2020)
"Some shoppers, though, see straight through vague labels and discount them entirely."
Lee (2022)

Step 2 · Build the woven sentence

Finish Step 1 to unlock this.

One sentence, no "Source 1 says."

You've got two sources that agree (Smith, Jones) and one that pushes back (Lee). Pick the pieces that hold them together in a single sentence.

A verb for what the agreeing pair does with the claim
A connective to bring in the source that pushes back
Several studies [verb] that vague labels mislead shoppers (Smith 2021; Jones 2020), [connective] Lee (2022) cautions that some see straight through them.
One strong version

"Several studies find that vague labels mislead shoppers (Smith 2021; Jones 2020), though Lee (2022) cautions that some see straight through them."

find / show / report tell the reader the pair proved something; say is flat. though / but / however signal the pushback; and would hide it. That contrast, agreement then tension, is the whole sentence.

Step 3 · Now yours

Build the model sentence to unlock this.

Weave two or three of your own sources.

Under one theme from your own WorkNet: which sources agree, and which one complicates it? Write a single sentence that holds them together. No "Source 1 says."

Several sources [find/show/report] that ____ (insert citation here), though (insert competing citation here) complicates it by _____.

Why the verb matters

A precise verb tells your reader what a source did with a claim. "Smith finds" or "shows" says the source has evidence; "argues" says it's a position; "warns" flags a risk. A flat "says" tells the reader nothing about how much weight the claim carries.

Flat: Smith says "natural" is misleading.  Precise: Smith (2021) finds that shoppers misread "natural."

Synthesis

Putting sources into conversation and stating what they reveal together, instead of summarizing them one at a time. It's most of what a literature review does.

List: "Smith says… Jones says… Lee says…"  Synthesis: "Two studies agree that vague terms mislead shoppers (Smith 2021; Jones 2020), though Lee (2022) complicates it."
